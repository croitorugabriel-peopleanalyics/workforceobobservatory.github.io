This article explains why certified HR KPIs depend on more than naming conventions. A semantic layer becomes valuable when it binds metric logic, dimensional meaning, time basis and governance ownership into a reusable contract that travels across tools.

01 · Deep dive

A contract with users

Certification is a promise that a metric can be reused safely. That promise has to be specific: what the KPI measures, which population it includes, how time is handled and what caveats should accompany interpretation.

02 · Deep dive

The KPI technical card

A metric should have a technical card that exposes owner, logic, grain dependencies, approved filters and release status. This is the mechanism that allows trust to scale beyond the people who originally built the calculation.

03 · Deep dive

Measures versus columns

Columns are raw ingredients; measures are governed business statements. Treating them as interchangeable leads teams to rebuild key metrics locally and slowly diverge from the certified standard.

Apply it

Publish measures as first-class analytical products so downstream tools consume business meaning rather than raw arithmetic components.

04 · Deep dive

Coherent time logic

A semantic layer is only coherent if all certified metrics respect compatible time rules. Period boundaries, point-in-time logic and historical restatements should be defined once and reused consistently.

05 · Deep dive

Explainability beyond a score

Certification should improve transparency, not merely authority. Users should be able to inspect the definition, inputs and known caveats behind a KPI so governance supports understanding rather than replacing it.
