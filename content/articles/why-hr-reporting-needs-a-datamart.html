This article expands the LinkedIn carousel into a practical argument for why trusted workforce reporting starts with a governed analytical foundation. The goal is not to eliminate spreadsheets, but to stop them from becoming the place where metric meaning, history and risk are silently redefined.

01 · Deep dive

The reporting trust problem

A spreadsheet can look finished while hiding unstable assumptions underneath it. When each monthly pack rebuilds population logic, reference dates or attrition rules by hand, leaders are not reviewing workforce reality—they are reviewing whichever local interpretation won that cycle.

02 · Deep dive

What an HR DataMart is

An HR DataMart creates a durable analytical layer between operational HR systems and executive reporting. It preserves agreed grain, standardized dimensions, historical context and reusable KPI logic so every downstream chart inherits the same definition instead of reinventing it.

Apply it

Agree the first release around the handful of decisions that repeatedly create reconciliation effort: headcount, movement, hiring, attrition and organizational structure.

03 · Deep dive

Common KPI definitions

The hardest reporting disputes rarely come from visualization. They come from hidden choices about who is included, when the measurement is taken and which events qualify. Phase 3 content makes those choices explicit because a KPI is only trusted when its contract is visible to business users and data teams.

04 · Deep dive

Historical analysis

Workforce decisions often depend on knowing what was true at a specific point in time. If corrections overwrite history or if extracts capture only the latest state, trend analysis becomes storytelling without evidence.

Apply it

Record effective dates, reporting dates and correction logic separately so analysts can explain whether a change reflects the workforce, the source system or a data repair.

05 · Deep dive

Visible data quality

Executives do not need every technical test, but they do need clear signals when a number should be treated with caution. A premium reporting platform surfaces freshness, completeness and rule status before the metric is used in a board conversation.

06 · Deep dive

Controlled access

Sensitive workforce measures often mix personally sensitive fields with leadership-grade aggregates. Access design should be part of the reporting architecture from the start, otherwise teams compensate later with manual workarounds that erode trust.

07 · Deep dive

From reporting to decisions

Once the data product contract is stable, reporting stops being a monthly assembly task and starts becoming a decision system. The same trusted core can support scorecards, narratives, deep dives and future AI-assisted experiences without redefining the basics each time.
