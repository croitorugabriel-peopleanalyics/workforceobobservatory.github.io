This article expands the LinkedIn presentation into a practical framework for People Analytics leaders, practitioners and data teams. It focuses on the choices that make workforce information trusted, explainable and decision-ready.

01 · Deep dive

The reporting trust problem

When teams repeatedly extract, clean and interpret the same records, each report can establish its own population, reference date and KPI logic. Reconciliation becomes recurring work and management attention moves from the decision to a debate about which number is correct.

02 · Deep dive

What an HR DataMart is

An HR DataMart is a decision-oriented analytical layer. It integrates selected operational data, preserves historical context, standardises keys and classifications, applies controls and exposes reusable business definitions. It organises source systems for analysis rather than replacing them.

Apply it

Document the rule, assign an owner, define a threshold or acceptance condition, and specify what users see when the control fails.

03 · Deep dive

Common KPI definitions

Common KPI definitions must be treated as an explicit part of the analytical product, not as an undocumented implementation detail. Define the business purpose, population, time basis, owner, control and failure behaviour. Then connect the rule to evidence that can be reviewed when data, structures or stakeholder expectations change. In the context of why hr reporting should not start with a spreadsheet, this is what makes the outcome reusable, auditable and explainable.
