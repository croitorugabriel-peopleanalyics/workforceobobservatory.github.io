This article starts the HR DataMart series with a design principle: begin with decisions, not tables. A workforce platform becomes valuable when it helps leaders make repeatable decisions under clear business rules, not when it simply centralizes every available field.

01 · Deep dive

Decision → Question → KPI → Data

The order matters. Teams should first define the business decision, then the question that informs it, then the KPI that clarifies the answer, and only then the data required to calculate it. Reversing the sequence usually creates large but low-value models.

02 · Deep dive

The data product contract

Every priority decision should have a visible contract: who uses the insight, what action window it supports, what metric interpretation is allowed and what caveats must remain attached. That contract prevents the architecture from drifting toward generic storage.

Apply it

If a decision cannot be named clearly, it is too early to model the supporting fact table. Clarify the business action first.

03 · Deep dive

Layer responsibilities

A decision-first model still needs disciplined technical separation. Sources capture transactions, pipelines standardize them, the model preserves analytical structure and the semantic layer publishes decision-ready meaning.

04 · Deep dive

Scope and minimisation

Enterprise ambition does not require day-one scope expansion. In Phase 3, high-quality content should reinforce that the first release can stay intentionally narrow if it proves one meaningful decision path end to end.

05 · Deep dive

The technical stop/go gate

Before a decision metric becomes operational, the team should know the failure conditions: missing source data, low completeness, stale hierarchy mappings or uncertified logic changes. A premium platform tells users when the answer should not be trusted.
