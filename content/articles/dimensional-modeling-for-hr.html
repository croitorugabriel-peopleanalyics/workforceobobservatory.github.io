This article translates dimensional modelling into workforce analytics language. The objective is not modelling purity for its own sake, but a structure that allows headcount, movement, hiring and organizational questions to remain comparable across reports and time.

01 · Deep dive

Declare the grain

Grain is the first promise a fact table makes. If the team cannot say whether a row represents a worker-day, a monthly snapshot, a movement event or a requisition event, every downstream KPI becomes vulnerable to accidental distortion.

02 · Deep dive

Separate facts and dimensions

Facts store measurable business events or states; dimensions carry the descriptive context that should be reused consistently. Keeping that separation clear is how the platform avoids duplicating organizational meaning in every analysis.

03 · Deep dive

Useful HR fact patterns

Workforce analytics typically mixes snapshot facts for stock questions and event facts for movement questions. The model should support both explicitly rather than forcing one pattern to behave like the other.

Apply it

Model headcount, hires, exits and organizational changes as separate analytical behaviors when they serve different decisions.

04 · Deep dive

Conformed dimensions

Manager, cost center, geography and role family should mean the same thing across every subject area. Conformed dimensions are what make cross-domain questions possible without renegotiating definitions report by report.

05 · Deep dive

Predictable relationships

The best model makes common joins unsurprising. Predictable relationships lower analytical risk because teams can explore, compare and extend the platform without inventing bespoke linkage logic each time.
